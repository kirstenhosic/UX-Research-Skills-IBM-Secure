# Research findings report

A 3–5 page body plus appendix, findings-first: the reader gets the answer
before the evidence. Everything in it renders from passed
[findings records](07-findings-record.md); if a claim, quote, or count is not
in a record, it does not go in the report. §4.8 of `EVALUATION-LOOP.md` is
the Definition of Done, and `skills/research-findings-report` carries the
full section recipes if you want more depth on any part.

Participants are P-IDs only, always. The one permitted edit inside a
verbatim quote is a bracketed substitution such as [P4] replacing a name.

## Structure

1. **Title page** — product, "UX Research Findings & Recommendations,"
   contributors with roles, date, and the declared destination tier.

2. **Executive summary** (≤ half a page) — the headline finding in the first
   sentence, the decision it informs, then the featured findings as
   one-liners, each with its count and confidence. This section concludes;
   it never restates. A reader who stops here leaves with the answer, and
   the summary would be wrong if the findings were different.

3. **Method note** (≤ a third of a page) — who, how many, what method, when,
   and the one or two limits that most constrain the findings. It sits high
   because a skeptic looks for it before granting the findings anything.
   Full method detail goes to the appendix.

4. **Findings** — the featured 3–6, ordered by importance, never by
   chronology or RQ number. Each finding: the claim as a heading, evidence
   with exact counts and one verbatim quote, your interpretation marked as
   yours, then confidence and limits in your own voice. The strongest
   finding gets the most room; equal-sized sections for unequal evidence is
   a lie told through layout.

5. **Recommendations** — numbered; each an action with an owner, its
   depends_on finding IDs, its horizon label, and its confidence. Rendered
   as a table so the room can scan it.

6. **What this means for you** (optional, ≤ a third of a page) — one or two
   lines per audience (PM, Eng, Design, customer-facing) when the report
   travels beyond the immediate team. Skip for a small, close team.

7. **Reviewer notes** — the flags that rode along: unmapped findings
   retained, research questions left unaddressed, open judgment calls. This
   is where the study stays honest in public.

8. **Appendix** (summarized in the document, full content in a companion
   file) — all findings records including non-featured ones, the RQ coverage
   matrix, method detail, participant profile, disconfirming evidence,
   alternatives considered per recommendation.

9. **Additional materials** — a linked list: each entry a name, one line on
   what it is and when to open it, a location, and an access note where
   restricted.

## Writing rules that do the most work here

`VOICE-AND-STYLE.md` is the standard; its rubric is what the readability
gate scores. The high-leverage items for a report: every quantifier exact
("5 of 8," never "most"); at least one verbatim quote per major finding; at
most one hedge per claim; active voice with named actors; confidence and
limits stated in your own voice, not only in a table; and no sentence
interrupted by an em dash or en dash.

## Formatting

Suite documents render in the IBM Carbon scheme (Blue 60 headings, Gray 100
body, IBM Plex Sans). A manual report in the team's standard document
template is fine; the structure above is what matters.

## Before release

Run the gates in order: safety (at the declared destination tier), synthesis
(every claim against records), readability. Then the release sign-off (§11):
you read the whole output and made your own edits, and it stays a draft
until you have. Edits that move a quote, count, or attribution re-run the
synthesis check.
