# Discussion guide / interview script

The guide is gated the moment it is drafted (§4.6 of `EVALUATION-LOOP.md` is
the full bar; `research-guide-checker` scores against it). This template
carries the structure and the rules that do the most work. The per-method
files in `methods/` add session shape and question counts for your specific
method; read yours before writing.

```
DISCUSSION GUIDE — [Study name]

Method:          [e.g., semi-structured interview / moderated usability]
Session length:  [minutes]
Guide type:      verbatim script / semi-structured / topic list
                 (severity of the gate scales with this; say which you mean)
Maps to:         RQ1, RQ2, ... (every core section names its RQ)
```

## 1. Introduction & consent (≈5 min)

Who you are, purpose in participant language, recording consent captured
out loud, "no wrong answers," and permission to stop any time. Confirm
consent scope matches what the tracker says this participant agreed to.

## 2. Warm-up (≈5 min)

Easy, contextual, zero-stakes questions. Nothing touching competence,
mistakes, or workarounds sits here (§4.6 item 20). Broad before narrow.

## 3. Core sections

One block per topic, each labeled with its research question. For each
section write main questions, follow-ups, and probes; a guide that is all
main questions fails the gate (item 9).

Question craft, the short version:

- **Open with TED+W:** Tell me about, Explain, Describe, Walk me through.
  A question that genuinely opens this way is hard to make leading,
  compound, or yes/no.
- **Behavioral before attitudinal.** Every topic is reachable through at
  least one question about a specific past instance (item 13), with the
  recall window bounded by recency or a landmark event (item 14). Ask what
  happened, not why they think they did it (item 5).
- **No leading, presupposing, or double-barreled questions** (items 2–3).
  Any yes/no question carries a probe (item 8).
- **Hypotheticals are not the only route to any topic** (item 4). When used,
  mark the resulting data as stated preference, not behavior.
- **Keep evaluative words out of questions.** Easy, hard, confusing,
  intuitive, seamless: the participant supplies those words; you probe them
  when they do (item 11).

```
SECTION: [topic]  (RQ2, ≈10 min)

  MAIN: Walk me through the last time you [specific task].
    FOLLOW-UP: What happened right before that?
    PROBE: You said it was [their word]. What does [their word] look
           like in practice?

  MAIN: ...
```

### Usability tasks (if applicable)

Each task carries a user goal, lean realistic context, the information the
participant needs, and a reachable end state. Task wording names no control,
presupposes no desire to perform it, and carries no loaded framing. In a
measured session, no probes are written into the task sequence (item 10).

## 4. Wrap-up (≈5 min)

Anything they expected to be asked; anything to add; thanks; what happens
next (mention that a findings follow-up may come, which the impact message
later fulfills).

## 5. Moderator reminders block (required, item 12)

The one lever the process has on live moderation:

- The always-probe list: subjective and evaluative language.
- Going off-guide is allowed; open with TED+W when you do.
- Mirror their words; do not translate them.
- Pause after the participant stops speaking; the second answer is often
  the real one.
